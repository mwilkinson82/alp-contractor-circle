## Slide 9: The Formulaic Advantage
**Why Systematized Trades Command Premium Valuations**

Here's the insight that matters for everyone in this room:

The contractors that get acquired at the highest multiples are not necessarily the ones doing the most complex work. They're the ones doing the most **repeatable** work.

Roofing. HVAC. Garage doors. Windows. Siding. Plumbing service calls.

Why? Because formulaic work means:
- A brand can be created around it
- Leads can be cultivated through marketing (not the owner's rolodex)
- New technicians can be trained on a documented process
- Margin capture is consistent and predictable
- The business scales without the owner on every job

This is why a roofing company with $10M in revenue and strong systems can sell for 7x to 9x EBITDA — while a custom builder doing $10M might sell for 3x or not at all.

Source: Profitability Partners, March 2026

## Slide 10: Roofing — A Case Study in PE Roll-Ups
**56 PE-Backed Roofing Platforms Are Actively Acquiring Right Now**

Roofing valuations by company size:

| Revenue | Typical Multiple |
|---|---|
| Under $3M | 3x – 5x EBITDA |
| $3M – $10M | 5x – 7x EBITDA |
| Above $10M (strong growth) | 7x – 9x EBITDA |

Key insight: **Storm revenue is discounted.** Buyers value storm/insurance work at only 0.5x to 0.7x the multiple they apply to base revenue. If 40% of your revenue is storm work, your blended multiple drops from 7x to about 5.5x.

Why? Because storm revenue is unpredictable. PE firms want recurring, predictable cash flow.

Owners who spend 12 months preparing for a sale achieve **30% to 50% higher total proceeds.** On a $10M roofing company, that preparation premium is worth $3M to $5M.

## Slide 11: What PE Firms Actually Look For
**The 7 Things That Make Your Company Acquirable**

1. **Owner-Independent Operations** — Can the business run without you for 90 days? If not, you don't have a company — you have a job.

2. **Recurring / Predictable Revenue** — Service agreements, maintenance memberships, government contracts. Top firms have 28% of revenue from recurring sources.

3. **Systematized Work** — Documented processes that any trained person can execute. That's how you scale.

4. **W-2 Workforce** — A company with 15 experienced W-2 installers is worth meaningfully more than the same revenue running entirely on subcontractors.

5. **Clean Financials** — Third-party verifiable EBITDA. Zero tolerance for books that can't be backed up.

6. **Brand-Driven Lead Generation** — Marketing produces customers, not the owner's phone calls.

7. **Documented Processes** — New people can be plugged in and produce results immediately.

## Slide 12: What Kills a Deal
**The Red Flags That Destroy Your Valuation — Or Kill the Deal Entirely**

- **Owner is the business** — If you're the primary salesperson AND hold all vendor relationships, buyers see the company as "un-transferable." Expect a **20-30% discount** or a heavy earn-out.

- **No recurring revenue** — If membership/service agreement revenue is below 15%, the asset is considered "high-volatility."

- **High turnover** — Technician turnover above 20% = toxic culture signal. Makes post-acquisition scaling impossible.

- **1099 misclassification** — Department of Labor audits are up. This is a hidden liability that can blow up a deal post-close.

- **Outdated tech / no systems** — If your business can't feed real-time data into a dashboard, you're a "fixer-upper," not a platform.

- **Aging workforce with no pipeline** — If your best guys are 5 years from retirement and you have no apprenticeship program, the buyer is acquiring a ticking clock.

Source: CFOx Advisory, March 2026

## Slide 13: The Labor Crisis Is Driving Acquisitions
**They're Not Just Buying Companies — They're Buying People**

The construction labor crisis is one of the biggest drivers of M&A activity:

- **80%+ of contractors** reported difficulty hiring in 2025 — highest rate in 3 years
- **40% of contractors** increased base pay faster than the prior year
- **110,000 licensed technician deficit** in 2026

The result: PE firms are executing "acqui-hires" — buying companies primarily to harvest their skilled workforce. Your master plumbers, your licensed electricians, your experienced foremen — those people are assets on a balance sheet.

This is why subcontractor M&A rose **38.6% in 2025** to 366 transactions — 65.1% of all construction deals.

Sources: AGC of America, CFOx Advisory, Capstone Partners

## Slide 24: The Accountability Chart
**Not an Org Chart — An Ownership Chart**

The Accountability Chart shows OWNERSHIP of 5 major functions:

1. **Visionary / CEO** — Culture, big relationships, big ideas
2. **Integrator / COO** — Runs the day-to-day, holds people accountable
3. **Sales / Marketing** — Generates revenue
4. **Operations** — Delivers the work
5. **Finance** — Manages the money

**ONE OWNER PER FUNCTION.** If two people own it, nobody owns it.

Most contractors sit in ALL 5 seats. That's why they work 70 hours a week and the business can't grow past them. That's why PE firms won't touch them.

---

## Slide 25: Accountability Chart — Contractor Example
**From 4 Seats to 1 — The Path to Owner Independence**

**Example: $3M Remodeling Company**

The owner currently sits in: Visionary + Operations + Sales + Finance = 4 seats. Working 70 hours/week.

**The Fix (over 1-3 years):**
- Year 1: Hire someone to take over Operations OR Sales → Owner goes from 4 seats to 3
- Year 2: Hire for the next function → 3 seats to 2
- Year 3: Hire an Integrator → Owner sits in Visionary only

You don't need to fill all 5 seats on day one. You need to SEE all 5 seats and have a plan to fill them.

**The market value impact:** A company where the owner sits in 1 seat is worth 2-3x more than a company where the owner sits in 4 seats. That's not opinion — that's what buyers pay.

## Slide 26: The People Analyzer
**Remove Emotion from Personnel Decisions**

Every person scored on two dimensions:

**Dimension 1 — Core Value Fit:**
- (+) = Embodies it
- (+/-) = Sometimes
- (-) = Violates it
- More than one (-) = wrong person. Period.

**Dimension 2 — GWC:**
- **G**et it — Do they understand the role?
- **W**ant it — Do they actually want to do it?
- **C**apacity — Do they have the skills, time, and emotional bandwidth?

All three must be YES. If any one is NO, they're in the wrong seat.

This replaces "I feel like he's doing okay" with clear, objective evaluation.

**The Framework:**

The goal: Every seat on your Accountability Chart filled with a Right Person who GWCs the role. That's how you build a team that runs without you.
